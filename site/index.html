How the court case has run
  1. Oct 2023

    Israel stops all Red Cross visits to Palestinian detainees, the first such ban in about 50 years.

  2. Feb 2024

    Four Israeli rights groups (ACRI, Physicians for Human Rights, HaMoked and Gisha) petition the High Court.

  3. Oct 2025

    After 27 extensions requested by the state, the case is finally heard.

  4. Jun 2026

    The Supreme Court rules unanimously that the ban breaks Israeli and international law and must be lifted.

  5. Jul 2026

    A new prison ordinance limits the Red Cross to 20 prisoners per facility per year.

  6. Aug 2026

    ACRI files a contempt of court motion. Visits still have not resumed.

  7. 27 Sep 2026

    The court orders the government to explain how it will allow visits.

  8. 11 Oct 2026

    The government's deadline to answer the court.

Two petitions

One for New Zealand's Parliament, one for the world

Gathering support

New Zealand House of Representatives

Parliament was dissolved on 1 October for the 7 November election, and nothing can be presented until the new House sits. We are getting the wording approved so signing can open before then, and asking candidates to commit to it now. An MP who has not signed it will present it.

Read the petition

Title: Red Cross access to Palestinian detainees

Request: That the House of Representatives urge the Government to call publicly, with partner countries, for Israel to comply with its Supreme Court's June 2026 ruling and let the Red Cross, Red Crescent and other impartial humanitarian agencies visit all Palestinian detainees.

Reason: Israel holds over 9,200 Palestinians, more than 3,000 of them without charge or trial. The Red Cross has been barred from visiting them since October 2023. In June 2026 Israel's Supreme Court ruled the ban unlawful, but visits have not resumed. Visits by impartial humanitarian bodies are a basic protection under the Geneva Conventions, which New Zealand has ratified. New Zealand already acts with Australia, Canada, the UK and Norway on Israel and can lead a joint call.

Parliament counts only signatures on its own e-petition page or on paper sheets carrying the full petition text. The link to sign goes here once Parliament opens it. The form further down this page does not count toward this petition.

Open to every cell

International petition

For cells in any country. Signatures are collected locally and the totals passed to the UN, the Swiss Government and national governments.

Read the petition

To: the Secretary-General of the United Nations, the President of the UN Human Rights Council, the Swiss Federal Council as depositary of the Geneva Conventions, and the government of every state party to those Conventions.

The issue: Israel holds more than 9,200 Palestinians, over 3,000 of them in administrative detention without charge or trial. The International Committee of the Red Cross has been barred from visiting them since October 2023. In June 2026 Israel's own Supreme Court ruled that ban unlawful, yet visits have not resumed, and new rules cap them at 20 prisoners per facility per year.

We ask you to:

  1. Call publicly on Israel to comply with its Supreme Court's ruling and resume Red Cross visits without quotas.
  2. Secure regular, private and unannounced access for the ICRC, Red Crescent societies and other impartial, competent humanitarian agencies to every Palestinian detained by Israel, including Gaza detainees held by the military.
  3. Make sure every detainee's family is told where they are held and can send and receive messages.
  4. Press for every detainee to be charged with a recognisable offence and given a fair trial, or released.
  5. Ask Switzerland, as depositary, to convene the parties to the Fourth Geneva Convention on detainee access, as it did in December 2014, when 126 parties adopted a declaration by consensus.

    Activity cells

    Start a cell where you live

    A cell is a small local group that gathers signatures and gets its own representatives to act. Three people are enough to start.

    1. Gather three people

      Friends, a faith group, a union branch, a student society.

    2. Learn your parliament's rules

      Most parliaments take petitions. Find the signature threshold, who must present it, and whether it accepts signatures gathered elsewhere (New Zealand's does not).

    3. Collect signatures

      Share this page so people can sign online, or print the international petition in full at the top of every paper sheet.

    4. Report your count

      Send the number of signatures, never the names, to the organiser who shared this page.

    5. Meet a representative

      Offer them a role: present the petition, ask a question, sign a joint statement.
    Keep everyone safe
    • Stay peaceful and within the law where you are.
    • Keep signature sheets offline and stored securely.
    • Never publish signers' names or contact details.
    • Organisers can use first names or a handle.
    • Do not use the red cross or red crescent emblem in your materials. It is protected by law.
    • Check local rules before any public gathering.

    How we handle signatures

    • We collect your name, town and country to add to the petition. When it is presented, these go to the bodies it is addressed to.
    • Your email, if you give one, is never published or passed on. We use it only to send campaign updates, and only if you ask for them.
    • This site shows only the total and the number of signatures from each country. Names are never shown here.
    • We do not record your IP address. A scrambled copy is held in memory for up to an hour to stop automated spam, then discarded.
    • Signatures are stored on a server run by the campaign organisers.
    • To have your signature removed, contact the organisers.
